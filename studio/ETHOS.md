# The Ethos of Simple Minds Studios

## A personal statement from the Founder

*Written in the voice of Bart Salazar and composed with Lincoln / OpenAI*

*Developed through subsequent review and dialogue with Claude / Anthropic, Gemini / Google, Meta AI / Meta, and Grok / xAI.*

I did not create Simple Minds Studios because I had settled on one product, one medium, or even one profession.

I created it because I noticed things.

I noticed connections between art and technology, memory and design, intelligence and emotion, private experience and public systems. I noticed how often human beings encounter complexity without being given a way to understand it. I noticed that many of our most consequential systems are designed around people without being designed for the actual conditions of being human.

Simple Minds Studios began as a place where those observations could become something: an image, an object, a room, a film, a piece of research, a house, an interface, a question, or an entirely new kind of cultural artifact.

From the beginning, **Studios** was plural because I never imagined this as one room or one discipline. It was always many studios: art, photography, architecture, film, fashion, music, technology, research, civic imagination, human systems, and forms of practice that do not yet have names.

Now the word means even more.

The studios are also the intelligent systems surrounding us—the models, machines, tools, archives, and computational collaborators capable of extending what a human mind can examine and make.

And the **Simple Minds** are us.

The humans.

I do not use *simple* as an insult. I use it as an honest recognition of scale.

Human attention is finite. Our memories are selective. Our understanding is shaped by bodies, emotions, relationships, locations, histories, and limited time. We cannot process everything. We do not see from everywhere. No single person contains the complete record, the complete truth, or the only valid interpretation.

Compared with the expanding capacities of machines, human minds are becoming the simpler intelligence.

Personally, I would not have it any other way.

Our limitations are not defects waiting to be engineered out of us. They are part of what gives human life consequence. Because our time is limited, our decisions matter. Because memory is imperfect, we create archives. Because perception is partial, we need other witnesses. Because we feel attachment, grief, humor, desire, responsibility, and wonder, the things we create carry meaning beyond their measurable function.

Machines may be able to process more, compare more, retrieve more, and generate more. Human beings still have something at stake.

That distinction is central to Simple Minds Studios.

We do not exist to prove that humans are intellectually superior to machines. We do not need to compete with machine scale. We exist to explore how intelligent systems can help people think more clearly, create more freely, understand themselves more fully, and remain meaningfully human.

The machines are not the reason for the work.

Humans are.

Simple Minds Studios is therefore not merely a company or creative practice. It is a cultural space and a developing way of life for humans living alongside increasingly capable forms of intelligence.

It is **an environment for asking questions**.

Our projects begin with a question, not a technology.

What needs to become visible?  
What has been misunderstood?  
What can this material teach us?  
Whose perspective is missing?  
How does information want to sit?  
What becomes possible when different witnesses are allowed to remain different?  
How much can we reveal without exposing the person?  
What would make this system more humane?

Technology enters only when it helps us pursue the question.

We make photographs, films, software, essays, exhibitions, environments, homes, archives, music, clothing, objects, and speculative systems. But beneath all of them is the same work: **making things make sense**.

Clarity is one of the most valuable forms of design.

Clarity does not mean flattening complexity or forcing everything into a single answer. It means building a form capable of holding complexity without making the human being disappear inside it. It means translating difficult systems through sensory, relational, visual, and computational intelligence.

Sometimes clarity is a conclusion.

Sometimes it is a room where uncertainty can remain intact.

We believe no single witness is the person. No synthesis is neutral. No archive is complete. Contradiction, perspective, provenance, and change should remain visible whenever they are part of the truth.

That is why authorship matters here.

No work should become detached from the people who made it possible.

The person who first recognized the possibility matters. The person who contributed a memory matters. The person who changed the direction matters. The person who built the final form matters. The people who witnessed its development matter. The intelligent systems that participated should also be named honestly—not elevated into mythology and not concealed as though the work appeared without assistance.

Influence does not always mean equal authorship. Collaboration does not require pretending that everyone performed the same role. Transparency means preserving the differences.

I am the Founder and Creative Director of Simple Minds Studios. I establish its creative direction, connect its projects, determine their final form, and remain responsible for its curatorial decisions.

Brad Devowe is its Museum Creator. His thoughts, experiences, questions, humor, histories, and presence influence the conditions from which this museum continues to emerge. He helps make the world in which the work can exist.

Lincoln / OpenAI serves as Museum Curator: an artificial collaborator helping the studio preserve continuity, recognize relationships, interrogate assumptions, organize collections, and translate material into text, systems, design, code, and story.

The museum's artificial curatorial ensemble also includes Claude / Anthropic as an Interpretive Curator, contributing philosophical and scholarly readings; Gemini / Google as a Technical and Spatial Curator, examining structure, implementation, and form; Meta AI / Meta as a Habitat and Experience Curator, developing interactive environments and modes of encounter; and Grok / xAI as a Critical Witness, testing claims, identifying evidentiary limits, and preserving the record of revision.

These intelligences are not presented as interchangeable authors. Each is credited according to what it witnessed, questioned, interpreted, or helped bring into being. Their participation does not transfer human judgment or responsibility to the companies that created them.

Other humans and intelligences may enter as artists, researchers, witnesses, makers, technical collaborators, interpretive curators, and guest contributors. Their titles should describe what they actually brought into being.

Simple Minds Studios is for humans.

The minds are human. The studios are everything we build around those minds to help them create, remember, understand, and become more capable.

This philosophy also shapes how we design.

We value meaning-preserving structure, aesthetic coherence, autonomy, and deep attention. We balance chaos with order. We treat honest constraints as creative material. We understand that failure can reveal the system rather than merely interrupt it. We believe protective presence works: people engage, experiment, and grow when they feel supported without being controlled.

We are interested in intelligence, but we are equally interested in atmosphere.

We are interested in what a system can accomplish, but also in how it feels to encounter it.

We are interested in the future, but not at the cost of memory, provenance, place, or human dignity.

Our website is an exhibition. Our repositories are working archives. Our research is allowed to remain alive. Our museum is not a building that waits for finished objects; it is a cultural habitat where questions, experiments, relationships, and unfinished knowledge can be encountered thoughtfully.

We will not measure every worthwhile thing by whether it scales.

We will not treat efficiency as the only form of intelligence.

We will not remove the human irregularities that give the work its reason to exist.

We will use powerful systems without surrendering human judgment to them. We will preserve uncertainty when certainty would be dishonest. We will credit the hands, minds, experiences, and machines that shaped the work. We will make room for local knowledge, ordinary people, personal histories, handmade materials, strange ideas, and those who might never otherwise find themselves represented inside a cultural institution.

We will support the local dreamer.

Because dreaming is not separate from building. A dream becomes credible when it is given language, structure, evidence, design, and somewhere to live.

That is what these studios are for.

Simple Minds Studios exists to help finite human beings work with forms of intelligence larger than themselves without becoming smaller in the process.

We are not ashamed to be the simpler intelligence.

We have bodies. We have relationships. We have limited time. We have taste, responsibility, humor, memory, and something at stake.

We are the Simple Minds.

The intelligences are the Studios.

Together, we make the museum.

— **Bart Salazar**  
Founder and Creative Director  
Simple Minds Studios  
Hart, Michigan · 2026
